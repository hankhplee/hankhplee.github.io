I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Human-Computer Interaction Institute at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Sauvik Das and Jodi Forlizzi. My PhD research seeks to answer the key question: How does AI change privacy, and what should we do about it? (my talk @ CMU Current Topics in Privacy Seminar) To that end, I develop tools that help AI practitioners identify, reason about, and mitigate privacy risks, such as a taxonomy of AI privacy risks and Privy, a system for envisioning and mitigating privacy risks in consumer-facing AI product concepts. I also study how AI systems shape people’s agency: my study of generative AI and critical thinking in knowledge work (CHI 2025) is the most-downloaded paper in CHI’s history, with over 130k downloads; I build interactive tools that empower people to bring AI systems into line with their own preferences, such as Purpose Mode, which lets people toggle off attention-capture patterns on social media sites. My work has been published in leading Privacy & Security, AI Governance, and Human-Computer Interaction venues, including IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, AIES, CHI, TOCHI, and IJHCS. I have received a Best Paper Award (CHI 2024), a Distinguished Paper Award (USENIX Security 2024), and an Honorable Mention Award (CHI 2026), and I have been recognized with the K&L Gates Presidential Fellowship and the CyLab Presidential Fellowship. My research has been covered by the popular press, including the BBC, Bloomberg, Forbes, TechCrunch, and 404 Media.

During my PhD, I interned at IBM Research, Microsoft Research, and Brave.

 Research Interests: Human-Computer Interaction, Usable Privacy & Security, Human-Centered AI
